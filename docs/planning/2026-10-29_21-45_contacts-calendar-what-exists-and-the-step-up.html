02The one open question

Your two proposals agree on far more than they disagree — and the one real disagreement is not engineering. It is a single question with three written answers: who owns contacts?

Calendar owner TBD — must be named first Stalwart Stalwart is the candidate (unprobed)
Google's role Read-only inbound, NEVER receives a write SaaS = never the owner Holds the only real bytes today

Calendar is not the contested question: all three documents point at the same answer (Stalwart, on the same DAV boundary as the contacts mirror), and both proposals reject the same alternatives (the CRM growing an event journal; a second DAV server). What calendar lacks is not a decision but a measurement — no CalDAV probe has ever run against your deployment.

Why this blocks everything The constitution's principle 1 is "one truth owner per entity type, named before any new spoke." With contacts contested and calendar unnamed, every line of sync code is a bet on an unresolved question. Both proposals say this. They just pick different answers — each citing your own documents.

Option 1 · smallest step

Light the machine — the journal hub

Mesh A · both proposals' phases P0–P5 · pbx-artmann research doc's recommendation

Keep the ratified constitution exactly as written. The Kith journal becomes contacts truth in practice (not just on paper): run the Google OAuth ladder, fill the journal with the 139 staged people plus both accounts' contacts, switch on the CardDAV mirror, enroll a device. Calendar follows after the CalDAV probe: name Stalwart in the TBD cell, build the inbound leg. "Bi-directional" is honored as two one-way legs with exactly one writer each.

Choose it if you want contacts working on your phone and in the CRM this week, with zero constitutional change and the smallest new-code surface.

The price Two owners, two restore drills, forever — and the journal fills with contact truth that option 2 would then have to migrate. A phone can never fully leave Google, because the device write surface stays Google-shaped.
Option 2 · recommended

One hub — Stalwart owns both, Kith owns relations

Mesh B · SystemNix proposal's recommendation · pbx-artmann doctrine's end-state

Amend the constitution once: Stalwart becomes truth for both personal PII types (contacts + calendar), on one DAV boundary, with one backup unit and one restore drill. Kith demotes to what only a CRM can do — relations, deals, tasks — holding links to people, not copies of them. The gcontacts investment is not thrown away: its People client, OAuth loopback, multi-account registry, state handling and single-flight runner get re-pointed into one Google↔Stalwart adapter covering cards and events. The CardDAV out-mirror retires (the owner is the DAV server now).

Choose it if you accept one amendment and a few weeks of adapter work in exchange for the structurally simplest end-state — and the only one where a phone can eventually drop Google entirely.

The price A ratified decision gets retired (that is what amendments are for), and contact lineage moves from journal provenance (every change carries an actor) to store-level history plus adapter logs. Auditability is real but coarser.
Option 3 · the long game

Exit Google — own the surface, not just the truth

Terminal state · pbx-artmann doc's option C · a lifestyle decision as much as an engineering one

Run a bounded import window (Google → Stalwart for both entity types), then freeze Google as a read-only mirror you stop typing into. Devices speak DAV (CardDAV/CalDAV) or a sync client pointed at your store. The two-writers question doesn't get answered — it gets deleted: one writer total, read-only OAuth scopes everywhere, best security posture of the three.

Choose it if leaving Google is the actual goal and you will change device habits to get it.

The price Every device and every calendar-sharing relationship (family, work, attendees) currently assumes Google. Assistant scheduling, Meet links, and shared calendars don't follow you to DAV. This option is only honest as a destination reached through option 2 — jumped at directly, it usually breaks on calendar sociology, not on technology.

04Recommendation

Option 2 — one hub — entered through three decision-free moves, with option 1 as the documented fallback.

The argument is a timing argument, and it is the one thing neither proposal says out loud: option 2's migration cost is zero today, and it grows every day option 1 runs. The journal currently holds zero people contacts. The moment the 139 staged people and both Google accounts land in it (option 1's first phases), contact truth exists in a store that option 2 would then have to migrate. Decide before you fill, and the amendment costs a table cell. Decide after, and it costs a migration. The cheapest this decision will ever be is right now.

And the decision does not even have to be made blind, because the three moves that unblock every option are identical:

  1. CalDAV probe suite against the deployed pin in the demo VM — the CardDAV suite is the template. Converts "Stalwart can own calendars" from upstream marketing into a measured verdict in docs/probes/. Required by all three options.
  2. Google OAuth plumbing — GCP project, OAuth client in production publishing mode, one consent per account. Every option's adapter needs exactly this, read-only first.
  3. Stalwart PII provisioning + restore drill — declarative address book and calendar plan objects (the dialect is already proven on the mail side), plus one drilled restore of the store. Whichever option wins, Stalwart holds something personal after this week.

The gate (kill switch) If the CalDAV probe disappoints — scheduling, recurrence or sync-token semantics broken at the pin — option 2 is off for calendar, and you fall back to option 1 with nothing wasted: the probe, the OAuth plumbing and the Stalwart drills are all load-bearing there too. That symmetry is why this recommendation is safe.
What I am asking you to accept One constitutional amendment (journal → Stalwart for personal contacts; Kith keeps relations), coarser lineage than journal provenance, and a few weeks of adapter work before the first bidirectional byte. In exchange: one owner, one DAV boundary, one backup unit, one restore drill — and a door out of Google that option 1 never opens.
